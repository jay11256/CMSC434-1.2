About Kitchen Kompanion

Kitchen Kompanion helps a household manage its kitchen. Instead of each person separately tracking groceries, recipes, dietary restrictions, expiration dates, and shopping lists, the app combines these tasks into one system centered on the food that is actually in the house.

Users can add new groceries, update quantities as items get used, and see approximate expiration dates or when it may be time to restock. From that inventory, Koko the Cat recommends meals that match each person's skill level, or points out which extra ingredients a recipe still needs.

Who it's for

Anyone who uses a kitchen, shared or not, and wants more guidance with their food habits: finding recipes, planning meals, or keeping track of dietary restrictions.

That includes the people who shop and stock the fridge, the people who eat from the shelves without managing them, and children old enough to grab a snack but not old enough to track what they can safely have.

A college senior sharing a fridge with three roommates needs to know which food is theirs, which is shared, and which is off limits. An adult with a newly diagnosed digestive condition needs help finding recipes that fit what they can eat now.

A middle schooler home alone after school wants to know which snacks they can make without asking a parent or using dangerous equipment. A guardian cooking for a family of five needs help planning the week's meals and a reminder when groceries run low.

Why it matters

When kitchen information is scattered, food goes to waste, duplicate items get bought, and roommates argue over whose groceries are whose. Connecting inventory to shopping lists and recipe suggestions helps people use what they already have and see what they still need.

Privacy

The inventory is shared by design, since everyone who uses the fridge needs to see what's in it. Dietary preferences are not. Restrictions are tied to a single profile, are only visible while that profile is in use, and never require a reason.

The app tracks food, not people: no calorie counts, weight, or eating history. Child profiles enforce restrictions rather than log habits.
